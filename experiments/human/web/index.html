Resource Game

Study information and consent

You are invited to take part in a short online study about how people make decisions in a simulated economy. You will play a resource game for about 15 minutes, alongside computer-simulated players, and answer a short questionnaire afterwards.

What you will do. Choose how much to "consume" each round from a shared pool of materials, for 30 rounds, then answer 5 short questions.

Payment. You will receive a flat fee for completing the study, plus a bonus based on your score in the game. Full amounts are stated on the study listing before you start.

Risks. This study poses no more than minimal risk. It is not a test of intelligence or ability, there are no right or wrong personal answers, and no health, financial, or otherwise sensitive information is collected from you.

Data. We record your in-game choices, the times you took to decide, and your questionnaire answers. We do not collect your name, email, or any directly identifying information — you are identified only by an anonymous participant code. Only the anonymised dataset is shared publicly as part of the research record.

Voluntary participation. Taking part is voluntary. You can stop at any point by closing the browser tab. You can request that your data be deleted within 14 days of taking part by contacting the researcher at the email on the study listing, quoting your participant code.

Welcome

You are about to play a 30-round resource game together with 19 computer-controlled players, in a shared economy of 20 households.

Every round, four materials — A, B, C and D — are available in a shared pool. Every household, including you, can "consume" materials at one of four levels: 0, 1, 2, or 3. Higher levels use more materials and score more points, but the materials are limited and regenerate on their own between rounds — if too much is taken, there is less available later.

You will also earn a small amount of cash each round (your "wage"), which you spend to consume. Depending on your assigned game version, you may also receive stamps — a separate kind of allowance, one stamp type per material — that you need alongside (or instead of) cash to consume materials.

Your total score is the sum of your points across all 30 rounds. Points convert directly into your bonus payment.

Cost. Each level costs cash, and — in some game versions — stamps of each material type. The cost of each level is shown on your screen every round. If you don't have enough cash (and stamps, if used), that level is greyed out.

Materials run out. If a material's shared pool runs very low, even an affordable level may deliver less than its usual points, because there isn't enough of that material to go around. A bar for each material (A–D) shows how healthy its pool currently is, from empty (red) to full (green).

Investing

At any point, you may spend 4.0 cash to invest in efficiency. This permanently reduces how much material your future consumption uses (by 15% each time, though the benefit shrinks with a floor). Investing does not add points directly, but may keep future levels affordable and pools healthier for longer.

Each round you will see

The round number, your cash (and stamps, if used), a health bar per material, the cost of levels 0–3, and the points you scored last round. There is no "undo" once you submit a round.

Thank you — here's what this study was really about.

You just played one of four versions of the same game. Some participants played with only cash ("scalar money"); others also needed material-specific stamps ("vector money"). Some played in a stable environment; others in one where the materials' regeneration weakened over time and occasional shocks destroyed part of two of the four pools. We are comparing how these different rule-sets affect what people do and how well the shared materials and everyone's scores hold up over time. Your individual data will only ever be reported anonymously and combined with many other participants' data.
