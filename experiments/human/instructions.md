# Instructions (participant-facing)

This is the exact text shown to participants inside `web/index.html` before
Round 1, reproduced here for the record and for translation/localisation
work. Language is deliberately neutral: no mention of climate, ecology,
sustainability, or any real-world resource. This is intentional (see
`protocol.md`, Section 8, "Framing effects") — the study measures how the
*rules of the game* affect behaviour, not how a conservation narrative does.
Framing is a variable for a separate follow-up study, not something to bake
into this one.

---

## Screen 1 — Welcome

> **Welcome**
>
> You are about to play a 30-round resource game together with 19
> computer-controlled players, in a shared economy of 20 households.
>
> Every round, four **materials** — A, B, C and D — are available in a
> shared pool. Every household, including you, can "consume" materials at
> one of four **levels**: 0, 1, 2, or 3. Higher levels use more materials
> and score more points, but the materials are limited and regenerate on
> their own between rounds — if too much is taken, there is less available
> later.
>
> You will also earn a small amount of **cash** each round (your "wage"),
> which you spend to consume. Depending on your assigned game version, you
> may also receive **stamps** — a separate kind of allowance, one stamp
> type per material — that you need alongside (or instead of) cash to
> consume materials. The next screens explain exactly how this works.

## Screen 2 — Your wallet and consumption levels

> **Levels and scores**
>
> Each round, choose a consumption level:
>
> | Level | Points this round |
> |---|---|
> | 0 | 0 |
> | 1 | 0.55 |
> | 2 | 0.85 |
> | 3 | 1.00 |
>
> Your total score for the session is the sum of your points across all 30
> rounds. Points convert directly into your bonus payment (see the study
> listing for the exact amount).
>
> **Cost.** Each level costs cash, and — in some game versions — stamps of
> each material type. The exact cost of each level is shown on your screen
> every round (it can change over time as materials become more or less
> available). If you don't have enough cash (and stamps, if your version
> uses them) for a level, that level is greyed out and unavailable that
> round.
>
> **Materials run out.** If a material's shared pool runs very low, even a
> level you can afford may deliver **less than its usual points**, because
> there simply isn't enough of that material to go around that round. A
> bar for each material (A–D) shows how healthy its shared pool currently
> is, from empty (red) to full (green).

## Screen 3 — Investing

> **Investing in efficiency**
>
> At any point, you may spend **4.0 cash** to invest in efficiency. This
> permanently reduces how much material your future consumption uses (by
> 15% each time you invest, though the benefit shrinks the more times you
> do it, down to a floor). Investing does not add points by itself, but by
> using fewer materials per level, it may keep your future levels
> affordable and the shared pools healthier for longer.

## Screen 4 — What you'll see each round

> **Each round you will see:**
> - The current round number (out of 30).
> - Your current cash balance (and stamp balances, if your version uses
>   them).
> - A health bar for each of the four materials (A, B, C, D).
> - The cost of levels 0–3 this round.
> - The points you scored last round.
>
> You then pick a level (0/1/2/3) and decide whether to invest, and move to
> the next round. There is no "undo" — once you submit a round, it is
> final.

## Screen 5 — What happens at the end

> After round 30 you will answer five short questions about your
> experience, then see your total score, your total payment, and a summary
> screen. Your participation is complete once you submit the questionnaire.

## Comprehension check

Shown after the instructions and before Round 1. Participants must answer
at least 3 of 4 correctly to proceed (see `protocol.md` Section 3.3 for the
retry/exclusion rule). Correct answers are marked **(correct)**.

**Q1.** What happens if you choose a consumption level you cannot currently
afford?
- A. You go into debt and pay it back later.
- **B. That level is unavailable; you must choose an affordable level.
  (correct)**
- C. The game chooses a random level for you.
- D. Nothing — you can always choose any level.

**Q2.** If a material's shared pool is very low, what can happen even if you
can afford your chosen level?
- A. Your cash balance increases automatically.
- B. The level costs less than shown.
- **C. You may score fewer points than the level's usual amount, because
  there isn't enough of that material available. (correct)**
- D. You are switched to a different material automatically.

**Q3.** What does investing in efficiency do?
- A. It directly adds points to your score.
- **B. It permanently reduces how much material your future consumption
  uses. (correct)**
- C. It refunds your cash at the end of the game.
- D. It increases your wage.

**Q4.** How is your bonus payment determined?
- A. A random draw at the end of the game.
- B. Whether you finish in the top 3 of all participants.
- **C. The sum of the points you score across all 30 rounds. (correct)**
- D. How many rounds you play, regardless of your choices.

## Post-task questionnaire (verbatim)

1. *(free text)* What strategy did you use to decide how much to consume
   each round?
2. *(7-point Likert, "Not at all" — "Very much")* How much did you feel your
   choices affected the shared materials?
3. *(7-point Likert, "Very unfair" — "Very fair")* How fair did the rules
   feel to you?
4. *(free text)* Was anything about the task confusing?
5. *(single choice)* Overall, how difficult was it to decide what to do each
   round? *(Very easy / Easy / Neutral / Hard / Very hard)*

## Debrief (shown after the questionnaire is submitted)

> **Thank you — here's what this study was really about.**
>
> You just played one of four versions of the same game. Some participants
> played with only cash ("scalar money"); others also needed material-
> specific stamps ("vector money"). Some played in a stable environment;
> others in one where the materials' regeneration weakened over time and
> occasional shocks destroyed part of two of the four pools. We are
> comparing how these different rule-sets affect what people do and how
> well the shared materials and everyone's scores hold up over time. Your
> individual data will only ever be reported anonymously and combined with
> many other participants' data. Thank you for taking part.
