## 1. Strategy in five lines

We are not betting on one door. We are running a small portfolio, sized to PI bandwidth (this is a nights-and-weekends project against a full-time pharma-logistics-engineering job, not a lab): **two open-door applications going in now** — Anthropic Economic Futures (rolling) and Foresight Institute (deadline 30 Sept 2026) — because both accept independent researchers and both fund exactly this kind of small, falsifiable simulation work; **one application built and ready to fire the moment its window opens** — CAIF, the best substantive fit in the entire landscape (cooperative-AI framing of Section 5's LLM-agent results) but currently between calls; **two low-cost relationship probes** — a Hewlett Foundation Environment & Society (ESI) letter of inquiry and a Laudes Foundation contact-form outreach — that cost an afternoon each and either open a program officer conversation or close cleanly; and **one longer relationship-building pathway** — outreach to KR Foundation and P4NE through a DEAL-registered preprint and a cold but well-targeted introduction, because both are strong fits (7–8/10) but neither takes unsolicited cold applications, so the actual "ask" this year is a conversation and a DEAL profile, not a proposal. Everything else in the landscape (ERC, Horizon Europe, Sloan, AI2050, Wallenberg, Volkswagen Stiftung, SFF, Climate Change AI, USSEE, ClimateWorks, OpenAI Researcher Access) is tracked so no deadline is missed, but is explicitly not this quarter's active work — most are gated on a university partner we do not yet have (Section 4).

## 4. The eligibility gap, and the institutional-partner strategy

A blunt count from Section 2: of the eighteen items on the landscape table, **six are structurally closed to an independent researcher regardless of proposal quality** — ERC (needs a host), Horizon Europe (needs to join a consortium), Climate Change AI (needs an academic PI), Volkswagen Stiftung (needs a German host), Wallenberg (needs a Swedish host), USSEE (needs a cross-institution team of two). A further three (P4NE, KR Foundation, AI2050) are relationship- or nomination-gated in a way that a stronger written proposal cannot fix. That is roughly half the landscape, and it happens to include the largest amounts (ERC's €1.5–2M dwarfs everything else in the table).

This is not a proposal-writing problem; it is a partnership problem, and it is worth being explicit about rather than working around it silently. **A single credible university or lab partnership unlocks or strengthens at minimum: ERC eligibility, Horizon Europe consortium credibility, Climate Change AI's academic-PI requirement, the human-study IRB route (thesis §experiments/human/protocol.md §9.3 already names "university partnership" as the preferred ethics-review path over a commercial IRB), and general reviewer confidence at Hewlett, Sloan, and Laudes, all of whom show a strong institutional-affiliation preference even where it is not a hard eligibility rule.**

Candidate partner types, in order of thesis fit:

1. **Ecological-macro modelling groups** — Surrey's Centre for the Understanding of Sustainable Prosperity (CUSP) and ICTA-UAB's team behind the REAL project (Kallis, Steinberger, Hickel; €9.9M ERC Synergy, "post-growth macroeconomics") are the closest existing research communities to this thesis's Section 1 argument and Section 4 SFC-consistency roadmap item. A REAL-affiliated co-author or advisor would make an ERC or Horizon Europe pathway realistic rather than aspirational.
2. **Complexity-economics groups** — groups working in the DSK/LAGOM/agent-based-macro tradition cited in the thesis's lineage table (§"Lineage and nearest prior art") are a natural fit for the engine itself (Mesa-based ABM, SFC-consistency check already on the roadmap) and would strengthen any Horizon Europe consortium pitch.
3. **Experimental-economics labs with existing oTree/Prolific infrastructure** — a lab that already runs oTree studies removes the single largest cost and risk item in the human-study protocol (thesis Phase 3; `experiments/human/protocol.md` §5–6) and is the most direct route to satisfying USSEE's cross-institution-team requirement and to a university ethics-board review in place of the commercial-IRB fallback the protocol currently budgets for.

**Approach**: this is being pursued opportunistically, not as a formal hire or grant-funded search — the arXiv preprint and GitHub Pages site (Section 6) exist partly *as* outreach material, so that a cold email to any of the above groups can point to something concrete rather than an idea. No partner search is currently budgeted as a line item in any proposal; it is conversation-cost, not project-cost.

## 5. Publication and credibility venues

Credibility with funders and credibility with the field are the same variable here — nearly every program in Section 2 that shows a "strong institutional/track-record preference" is really asking "has this been peer-reviewed or has anyone else vetted it," which a preprint and a conference presentation answer more cheaply than a grant does.

- **Ecological Economics** — the natural home journal for this thesis's core argument (Section 1's Daly/Raworth/Weitzman synthesis). Its March 2026 special issue on "Ecological Macroeconomics Modelling" has already closed and was missed; **watch explicitly for a successor call or special issue**, and in the meantime treat a standard submission (not tied to a special issue) as viable once the design-space paper (thesis roadmap Phase 4) exists.
- **ISEE conferences** (International Society for Ecological Economics) — the right presentation venue for early results; a conference talk or poster on the thesis §4 first-results table is achievable now, independent of any paper being finished, and is a low-cost credibility step ahead of the Laudes/KR/Hewlett-style institutional funders who respond to conference presence.
- **arXiv (q-fin / econ.GN)** — preprint the current engine and first-results paper immediately (Section 6); this is the single highest-leverage, lowest-cost credibility action available this month, and is a prerequisite artifact for essentially every LOI and cold-outreach email in this strategy.
- **JASSS** (Journal of Artificial Societies and Social Simulation) — the right venue specifically for the *methods* paper once the four-substrate comparison (rule/RL/LLM/human, thesis §5) has enough data to be a paper in its own right; distinct from the Ecological Economics submission, which should carry the institutional-economics argument rather than the simulation-methodology one.

Low-cost, no-dependency actions that either unlock later steps in this strategy or cost nothing to attempt:

1. **Repo description and topics** — set the GitHub repository description and topics (e.g. `agent-based-modeling`, `ecological-economics`, `mesa`, `climate`, `llm-agents`) so the project is discoverable by the exact search terms a program officer or potential partner would use.

3. **arXiv preprint** — submit the thesis and first-results (docs/thesis.md §4) as a q-fin or econ.GN preprint. This is the single action most other items in Sections 2–5 depend on (citability for LOIs, a concrete artifact for cold outreach, a prerequisite for the Sloan/Hewlett LOI track, and a step toward both journal targets in Section 5).
4. **DEAL registration** — register the project/PI profile on DEAL as the calling-card mechanism for the P4NE and KR Foundation relationship pathway (Section 4), since neither takes unsolicited applications directly.
5. **OpenAI Researcher Access form** — submit this quarter's application for ~$1,000 in API credits; independent of the Anthropic-model work already budgeted elsewhere, this specifically funds the multi-model replication check that H4 (thesis §6) and the methodological cautions in thesis §5 (prompt-sensitivity, cross-model replication) call for, at effectively zero cash cost.
